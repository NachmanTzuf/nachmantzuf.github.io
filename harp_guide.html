1. Physics and Mechanics of Lever Harps

Core Mechanical Axiom: On a lever harp (Celtic or folk harp), sharping levers can ONLY raise the pitch of an open string by one semitone (+1 half step). A sharping lever can never flatten a string. Consequently, any key requiring flat notes can only be played if those strings were originally tuned flat in the open position (with the lever down).

A standard lever harp possesses seven diatonic strings per octave (C, D, E, F, G, A, B). At the top of each string near the bridge pin sits an individually actuated sharping lever (e.g., Loveland, Camac, Truitt, or Rees levers).

  • Lever DOWN (Disengaged / Open): The string vibrates freely along its full speaking length from the soundboard eyelet to the bridge pin. It sounds at its fundamental tuned pitch.
  • Lever UP (Engaged): The lever cam presses the string firmly against a fret pin, mathematically shortening the vibrating length by a semitone ratio of \(2^{-1/12} pprox 0.94387\). This raises the pitch of the vibrating string by exactly 100 cents (one half step).
  • String Transformation Rules:
    • If tuned to a Flat (e.g., E♭): Lever DOWN = E♭, Lever UP = E natural.
    • If tuned to a Natural (e.g., C): Lever DOWN = C natural, Lever UP = C♯.
    • Therefore, if a string is tuned natural open, it can never produce a flat pitch unless the harpist physically takes a tuning wrench and retunes the tuning pin down by a half step.

6. The 7 Diatonic Modes: Theory and Architectural Catalog

In modal ear training and harp performance, each major scale generates seven distinct musical modes depending on which scale degree serves as the tonal center (tonic). Each mode carries a unique emotional atmosphere, interval formula, and cadential resolution signature.

Ionian (Major) — 1st Degree of Parent Major
Formula: 1 - 2 - 3 - 4 - 5 - 6 - 7
Characteristic Interval: Major 3rd and Major 7th (bright, resolved, stable)
Diatonic Triads: I - ii - iii - IV - V - vi - vii°
Standard Modal Cadences: Authentic: V → I | Plagal: IV → I | Functional: I - IV - V - I
Aesthetic Mood & Tradition: Bright, triumphant, resolved, classical
Dorian — 2nd Degree of Parent Major
Formula: 1 - 2 - ♭3 - 4 - 5 - 6 - ♭7
Characteristic Interval: Natural 6th with Minor 3rd (avoids the melancholy of natural minor)
Diatonic Triads: i - ii - ♭III - IV - v - vi° - ♭VII
Standard Modal Cadences: Modal Signature: i - IV - i (or i - IV - v - i, or ii - V - i relative)
Aesthetic Mood & Tradition: Noble, medieval, bittersweet, Celtic ballad standard (e.g., Scarborough Fair, Drunken Sailor)
Phrygian — 3rd Degree of Parent Major
Formula: 1 - ♭2 - ♭3 - 4 - 5 - ♭6 - ♭7
Characteristic Interval: Minor 2nd (half step above tonic, exotic Spanish/flamenco gravity)
